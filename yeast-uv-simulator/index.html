Can UV light damage living cells? Can anything protect them?

Spread yeast on two petri dishes, choose a cover and a time in the sun, then incubate the dishes and count what grew.

Your two kinds of yeast

A UV-sensitive yeast

Scientists removed (“knocked out”) the gene that repairs DNA damage from UV. This is like a human cell with no working p53.

B Wild-type yeast

Normal yeast, like the kind found in nature. Its DNA repair works, like a human cell with working p53.

Yeast has only one cell, so it cannot form a tumor. If UV damages its DNA and the cell cannot repair it, the cell dies. Each dot (colony) on a dish grew from one cell that survived.

Set up a trial

Choose a cover, a time, and a prediction. Then run the trial.

  1. Spread 0.25 mL of yeast on each dish with glass beads.
  2. Cover the dishes and put them in the sun.
  3. Incubate upside down in the dark for 3 days.
