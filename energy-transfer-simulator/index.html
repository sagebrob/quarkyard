Energy Transfer Investigation Simulator

The real version of this investigation uses hot, warm, cool, and cold water baths, foam cups with lids, a thermometer, and a digital scale, worked through with a lab group at a station. This simulator runs the same liquid-liquid investigation on your own device, so you can complete it by yourself, at your own pace, even if you missed the day your class ran it. You set up and run one trial at a time — the results depend on the choices you make.

Built for one person — no lab partner or group needed
Investigation questionWhat affects how much energy transfers between warm and cool water when they are mixed together?

You're in charge of the setup. Choose a water bath and a mass of water for each cup, and choose an open or closed system. Then run the trial. When it finishes, change the setup and run the next one.

Each graph plots one cup as a point: its mass across, its change in temperature (ΔT) up or down. The shaded rectangle from the axes to that point has an area of mass × ΔT. For water, that area is the energy the cup lost or gained, in calories.

Glossary

System
The part of the world you're focused on studying — here, the warm water, cool water, and cup in one trial.
Open system
A system that can trade matter or energy with its surroundings. In this simulator, heat leaks between the water and the room air in the open system, like your real cups.
Closed system
A system where matter cannot get in or out, even though energy sometimes can (like a lidded cup or a sealed bottle).
Conduction
The transfer of energy through direct contact, as particles collide with one another.
Equilibrium
A stable state a system settles into — here, the single final temperature both water samples reach once energy stops flowing between them.
Heat (Q)
The name scientists give to energy that flows through conduction. Measured in calories in this investigation.
Calorie
A unit of energy. One calorie is roughly the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 gram of water by 1 °C.
Specific heat (c)
How much energy it takes to change the temperature of 1 gram of a substance by 1 °C. Every substance has its own value; water's is about 1 cal/g·°C.
Conservation of energy
Energy flows between objects but is not created or destroyed — what one sample loses, another gains (accounting for any that escapes the system).
Controlled variable
Something you deliberately keep the same across every trial, so it can't explain any differences in your results.
Independent / dependent variable
Independent: the one thing you deliberately change on purpose. Dependent: what you measure, because you expect it to change as a result.
