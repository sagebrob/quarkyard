Balloon Lung Model: Healthy vs. Tumor

Español

This is a model of the chest made from a bottle and balloons. Pull the diaphragm down on both models at the same time and compare how much air each pair of lungs takes in.

Purple patchTumor: stiff tissue that can't stretch

How breathing works

  1. When you breathe in, your diaphragm pulls down.
  2. That makes more room inside the chest.
  3. Air rushes in through the windpipe and the lungs stretch to fill the extra room.
  4. When the diaphragm goes back up, the air is pushed out.
  5. A tumor is stiff, so the part of the lung with the tumor can't stretch. With the same pull, less air gets in.

Build it with real balloons

You can make this model with simple materials. Ask your teacher before cutting anything.

  1. Cut the bottom off a plastic bottle. This is the chest.
  2. Put a straw or Y-shaped tube through the bottle's cap. Tape a small balloon to each end inside the bottle. These are the lungs.
  3. Seal the cap with clay or tape so air only gets in through the straw.
  4. Stretch a cut balloon or rubber glove over the open bottom and tape it tight. This is the diaphragm.
  5. Pull the bottom sheet down and watch the lungs fill. Then cover part of one lung balloon with a few layers of tape to make a tumor, and try again.
This is a simplified model. Real lungs are spongy, not balloons, and a healthy breath at rest is about 500 mL of air. The tumor's effect on air is estimated by the model, not measured from patients.
